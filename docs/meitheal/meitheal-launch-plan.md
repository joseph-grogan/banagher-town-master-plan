# Banagher Meitheal — Launch Plan

**Many Hands, One Community**

---

## What Is the Meitheal?

A meitheal (meh-hal) is an old Irish tradition — neighbours coming together to help one another and improve their community. The Banagher Meitheal is a modern, low-commitment version of that: a WhatsApp group of local people willing to lend a hand when they can.

No committees. No meetings. No obligations. Just a network that can be activated when a project needs a few extra pairs of hands.

---

## Why It Matters

The Banagher Town Masterplan lists 25+ community projects. Many of them — Tidy Towns, Fort Falkland, the Graveyard Signage project, the Photo Archive, community events — regularly need volunteers for short bursts of work. Right now there's no single, easy way to rally people.

The Meitheal is the engine that connects willing hands to real tasks. It's not just another project on the list — it's the thing that helps all the other projects move.

---

## How It Works

### The WhatsApp Group

- One group: **Banagher Meitheal**
- Used **only** for call-outs (not general chat)
- Anyone can join, no commitment expected
- If a call-out suits you, show up. If not, no problem.

### The Call-Out Format

Every call-out posted to the group should follow the same simple format:

> **📋 MEITHEAL CALL-OUT**
>
> **What:** Plant flower beds at the Marina entrance
> **When:** Saturday 20th September, 10am–12pm
> **Where:** Meet at the Marina car park
> **Bring:** Gloves if you have them — tools provided
> **Contact:** Johnny (087-XXX-XXXX)
>
> No signup needed — just show up!

Keeping call-outs consistent means people can scan them quickly and decide on the spot.

### Ground Rules (pinned in the group)

1. This group is for meitheal call-outs only — no replies, no general chat, memes, or politics. If you have a question, contact the coordinator directly.
2. Call-outs are posted by the coordinator (or project leads by arrangement).
3. There's never any pressure to attend. Show up when you can.
4. Be respectful and welcoming — especially to new faces.

## What We Need Before Launch

### 1. Appoint a Coordinator

One person who owns the rhythm for the first 3 months. Their job:

- Post call-outs to the group
- Liaise with other project leads (Tidy Towns, Fort Falkland, etc.) to source tasks
- Welcome new members with a short message and the ground rules
- Keep the energy going with a steady beat of events (aim for 1–2 per month)

**Suggested:** Johnny takes this on initially. If it works, someone else will naturally step up.

### 2. Line Up the First Event

Do not launch the group empty. Have the first meitheal ready to announce the moment people join. Something visible, achievable, and social.

**Ideas for a first event:**

- A tidy-up of a neglected corner of town (coordinate with Tidy Towns)
- Clearing or planting at Fort Falkland
- Help with setup for an upcoming community event
- A cleanup along the Marina or riverbank

The first event sets the tone. Pick something that:
- Takes 2 hours max
- Needs no special skills
- Has a visible before-and-after result
- Ends with tea/coffee and a chat

### 3. Create the WhatsApp Group

- Create the group, name it **Banagher Meitheal**
- Set the group description (use Option 1 text — see Appendix)
- Add a group icon (something simple — the town crest, a pair of hands, or the Meitheal tagline)
- Pin the ground rules as the first message
- Pin the first event as the second message
- Generate the invite link

### 4. Connect to Masterplan Project Leads

Reach out to the leads of projects that regularly need hands:

| Project | Why They Need Meitheal |
|---|---|
| Banagher Tidy Towns | Weekly/fortnightly cleanups, planting days |
| Fort Falkland | Event setup, maintenance |
| Graveyard Signage | Committee formation, physical work |
| Photo Archive | Collection drives, scanning sessions |
| Sionn Mhara | Launch events, crewing |
| Christmas Lights | Installation and takedown |
| Community Events | Setup, stewarding, teardown |

Ask each: *"What's one thing in the next month where extra hands would help?"* — that gives you a pipeline of call-outs.

## Keeping It Alive

The biggest risk is the group going quiet. Here's how to prevent that:

- **Steady rhythm:** Aim for 1–2 call-outs per month minimum. Even small tasks keep the group feeling active.
- **Show the impact:** After each event, post 2–3 photos and a short "here's what we did" message. People need to see results.
- **Rotate the source:** Don't let every call-out come from the same project. Variety keeps it interesting — one month it's planting, next it's event setup, next it's a heritage cleanup.
- **Welcome newcomers:** When someone new joins, a quick "Welcome! Here's what we're about" message goes a long way.
- **Celebrate milestones:** "10 meitheal events done!" / "50 members!" — small wins build momentum.
- **Review at 3 months:** Is it working? Does the coordinator role need to rotate? Adjust and carry on.
